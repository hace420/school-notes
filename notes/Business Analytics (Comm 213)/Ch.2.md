# Sampling methods

## Simple Random Sampling

- Every individual in the population has an equal chance of being selected
## Stratified Sampling:

- The researcher divides the population into subgroups, or strata, that share a similar characteristic, and then samples from each subgroup.

## Cluster Sampling

- The researcher divides the population into clusters, often based on geographic or organizational boundaries. A random selection of clusters is chosen, and then all individuals within the selected clusters are sampled.

## Convenience Sampling

- The researcher selects individuals who are easiest to reach or readily available.

## Snowball Sampling

- The researcher, initially, selects a small group of individuals, and then those individuals refer others who meet the study criteria. (Used when the population is hard to access or hidden).

# Frequency Distribution

**You need to follow these steps to create a frequency distribution:**

- 1
    
    **Collect the data:** This is the raw, unorganized data you've gathered.
    
- 2
    
    **Determine the range of the data:** Find the difference between the highest and lowest values. This helps us understand the spread of our data.
    
- 3
    
    **Divide the range into intervals (class widths):** Decide how wide each interval or 'bin' should be.
    
- 4
    
    **Count the frequency of data points in each interval:** Go through your data and count how many values fall into each interval. This count is the 'frequency'.
    
- 5
    
    **Summarize in a table:** Organize the intervals and their corresponding frequencies into a table.
