# Sample spaces 

- set of all outcomes is called a sample space
- Denoted by "S".

Experiment: Tossing a coin once.
– S = {H, T}
Experiment: Rolling a cubic die once.
– S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

**Two kinds of sample spaces DISCRETE and CONTINUOUS.**

**Discrete** --> can be counted (can be finite or infinite).
**Continuous**  --> the outcomes fill an entire region in the space (an interval of real numbers, measuring time)

# Events 

An event, E, is a **set of outcomes** of a probability experiment, i.e. a subset in the sample space.

– E ⊆ S. That is, **an event is a subset of S**

----> **If an event E contains no outcomes from S, then E is an impossible event.**

The union and the intersection of events are defined in a natural way (set operations), giving possibly new events.
– Union: E ∪ F (or)
– Intersection: E ∩ F (both)
– Complement: Eᶜ (not E)

Example: In the race among 7 horses experiment, if E = {all outcomes in S starting with a 3}, then E is the event that the number 3 horse wins the race.

**Two events, E and F, are called mutually exclusive, if**
EF = φ

EF is the intersection of sets E and F, and φ denotes an empty set)
• That is, it is impossible for an outcome to be an occurrence of both events.
– E.g., for the cast die experiment, events
E = {2,4,6} and F = {1,5} are mutually exclusive
