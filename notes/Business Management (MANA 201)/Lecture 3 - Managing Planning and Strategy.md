# Planning and Strategy

Planning → process that managers use to identify and select suitable goals and courses of action for an organization

## Why Planning is Important

- Direction & Purpose → what goals organization is trying to achieve and strategies to achieve them
- Participation → allows managers to participate in decision-making about appropriate goals and strategies
- Coordination → ensures all managers within organization all pull in same direction towards future desired state
- Control → specifies who is responsible for putting the strategies into action to achieve the goals

## Qualities of Effective Plans

- **Unity**: at any time only one central, guiding plan is put into operation to achieve an organizational goal 
- **Continuity**: an ongoing process in which managers build and refine previous plans 
- **Accuracy**: collect and use all available information in the planning process. 
- **Flexibility**: plans can be altered and changed if the situation changes

# Steps in the Planning Process (5)

![[Pasted image 20260928182833.png]]

## Step 1

- Vision Statement → Big picture of the organization and/or its dreams for future
- Mission Statement → Declaration of purpose that identifies the organization's products and customers and distinguishes the organization from competitors
- Values Statement → What the organization believes in and how people in the organization are expected to behave.
- Goals → desired future outcome within specified time frame

### Internal: SWOT

- Strengths - particular skills and resources to pursue goals effectively 
- Weaknesses - where it is lacking resources and prevented from pursing goals 
- Opportunities - conditions favorable to organization 
- Threats - conditions that prevent organization from achieving its goal

![[Pasted image 20260928183927.png]]

## Step 3

Business-Level Strategy 
- Plan to gain a competitive advantage in a particular market or industry 
-  Increase the value to customers and other stakeholders by either 
	1. Lowering the costs 
	2. Increasing product differentiation so that more customers want to buy the products even at higher or premium prices
#### “Stuck in the Middle”

According to Porter, managers cannot simultaneously pursue both cost-leadership and differentiation strategies

Organizations stuck in the middle have lower levels of performance

## Step 4

Five-Step Process: 
1. Allocating responsibility for implementation 
2. Drafting detailed action plans that specify implementation 
3. Establishing a timetable (with goals) for implementation 
4. Allocating appropriate resources 
5. Holding specific individuals or groups responsible for reaching corporate, divisional, and functional goals
