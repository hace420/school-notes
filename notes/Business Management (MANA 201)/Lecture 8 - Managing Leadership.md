# The Behavioural Model

*→ Identifies two basic dimensions of leadership behaviours:*

1. Consideration (employee-centered) behaviours: showing care, trust and respect towards subordinates 
- Emphasis on well-being of employees and interpersonal relations

2. Initiating Structure (production–oriented) behaviours: define and structure their role/ roles of their subordinates to achieve organizational goals 
- Emphasis on task-related and goal achievement behaviours, including technical aspects of job

# Situational Model of Leadership: Path-Goal Theory

*→ What can leaders do to motivate their subordinates to reach group & org. goals?* 

1. Find out what outcomes your subordinates are trying to obtain from their jobs and the organization 
2. Reward subordinates for high performance and goal attainment with the outcomes they desire 
3. Clarify the paths to goal attainment for subordinates, remove any obstacles to high performance, and express confidence in subordinates’ capabilities

## House’s Goal-Path Theory

![[Pasted image 20261109180559.png]]

## Path-Goal Theory

*→ Directive Behaviours:* setting goals, assigning tasks, showing subordinates how to complete tasks, and taking concrete steps to improve performance 

*→ Supportive Behaviours:* expressing concern for subordinates, looking out for their best interests 

*→ Participative Behaviours:* give subordinates a say in matters and decisions that affect them 

*→ Achievement-oriented Behaviours:* motivate subordinates to perform at highest level possible

#### Examples:

a. Highly motivated and skilled teacher is resisting changes to the curriculum 
*→ Participative leader behaviour*

b. New hire on an assembly line consistently makes errors      *→ Directive behaviour*

c. The Leader of a task force finishes writing the final report for a project 
*→ Achievement oriented leader behaviour*


# Visionary Models of Leadership

- Leaders are not only expected to perform supervisory tasks but also need to focus on *vison-setting activities*

**Visionary models of leadership:**
1. Transactional/Transformational leadership 
2. Charismatic leadership 
3. Turnaround leadership

## Transactional Leadership

*Transactional Leadership → managers motivate subordinates in direction of established goals*
→ Employees will generally meet (but not exceed) expectations

> - Based on rewards and punishment 
> - Contingent Reward Leadership 
> - Management by Exception


## Transformational Leadership

*Makes subordinates aware of the importance of their jobs and performance to the organization*

**Associated Behaviours:**

1. Idealized Influence (Charisma): leader acts as role model
2. Inspirational Motivation: Provides compelling vision 
3. Intellectual Stimulation: Creative problem solving
4. Individualized Consideration: Shows consideration for personal growth needs of subordinates

> Do people perform better under transformational leadership? Yes, they do!
