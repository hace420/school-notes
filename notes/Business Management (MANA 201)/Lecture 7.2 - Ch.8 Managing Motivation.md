## Need Theories of Motivation

- Need theories → in order to motivate a person, manager must: 
	 - Determine what needs the person is trying to satisfy at work 
	 - Ensure that a person receives the outcomes that help satisfy those needs when performing well

### McClelland’s Need for Achievement, Affiliation and Power

- *Need for Achievement* → how strong is one’s desire to perform challenging tasks well and to meet personal standards for excellence 
- *Need for Affiliation* → how concerned an individual is about establishing and maintaining good interpersonal relations, being liked, and having the people around him or her get along with each other 
- *Need for Power* → how concerned an individual is with their ability to control or influence others

## Process Theories of Motivation

> Process Theories: Explain the processes by which employee behavior can be aroused and then directed


### Expectancy Theory

*Three factors determine a person’s motivation:* 
	**→ Expectancy:** perception about the extent to which effort will result in certain level of performance 
	**→ Instrumentality:** perception about the extent to which performance at a certain level will result in receiving rewards 
	**→ Valence:** how desirable each of the outcomes/rewards available from a job or organization is to a person

![[Pasted image 20261102185335.png]]

- People will be motivated to perform in those work activities that they find attractive and that they feel they can accomplish. 
- The attractiveness of various work activities depends on the extent to which they lead to favourable personal consequences.

### Goal-Setting Theory

> Goal-Setting Theory: identifies types of goals most effective in producing high levels of motivation and performance

- Explains why goals have these effects 
- Goals must be specific and difficult 
- Managers should encourage workers to develop action plans to attain goals

### Management by Objectives (MBO)

> Management by Objectives (MBO): formal system of evaluating subordinates on ability to achieve specific organizational goals or performance standards

1. Specific goals and objectives are established at each level of organization 
2. Managers and subordinates together determine the subordinates’ goals 
3. Managers and their subordinates periodically review the progress toward meeting goals

### Learning Theories

> Learning Theories: focus on increasing employee motivation and performance by linking the outcomes that employees receive to the performance of desired behaviours and the attainment of goals


Several learning theories: 
1. Operant conditioning theory 
2. Social learning theory

#### Operant Conditioning Theory

> Operant Conditioning Theory (Reinforcement Theory): People learn to perform behaviours that lead to desired consequences and learn not to perform behaviours that lead to undesired consequences

Four tools to motivate: 
1. *Positive Reinforcement →* gives people outcomes they desire when they perform well 
2. *Negative Reinforcement →* eliminating undesired outcomes once people have performed organizationally functional behaviours 
3. *Extinction →* stopping the performance of dysfunctional behaviours by eliminating whatever is reinforcing them 
4. *Punishment →* administering an undesired consequence when dysfunctional behavior occurs
![[Pasted image 20261102190256.png]]


#### Social Learning Theory

> Social Learning Theory: Motivation results from direct experience of rewards and punishments and also from a person’s thoughts and beliefs


### Total Reward Strategy

> Total Reward Strategy: encompasses both intrinsically and extrinsically motivating factors such as:

- Career development opportunities 
- Base pay amounts/ Merit increases 
- Non-financial recognition - Employee recognition programs: management expressions of interest, approval and appreciation for a job well done 
- Employee development and training
