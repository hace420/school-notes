# Communication Skills for Senders

1. Send messages that are clear and complete
2. Encode messages in symbols the receiver understands and avoid jargon
3. Select a medium appropriate for the message
4. Select a medium that the receiver monitors
5. Avoid filtering (holding back information) and information distortion as the message passes through other workers
6. Ensure a feedback mechanism is included in messages
7. Provide accurate information to avoid rumours

#  Communication Skills for Receivers

1. Pay attention
2. Be a good listener
	✓Make eye contact
	✓Exhibit affirmative nods and appropriate facial expressions
	✓Avoid distracting actions or gestures
	✓Ask questions
	✓Paraphrase
	✓Avoid interrupting
	✓Do not overtalk
	✓Make smooth transitions between roles of speaker and listener
3. Be empathetic

# Choosing a Communication Network and Channel

Most appropriate choice depends on several variables:
- Level of **information richness** needed
- **Sensitivity** of information involved
- **Time** constraints
- Need for permanent **record**

![[Pasted image 20261011144311.png]]

## 1. Face-to-Face Communication

**Management by wandering around (MBWA):** face-to-face technique; manager walks around work
area and talks informally with employees

Video conferences capture some of these advantages:
• speed up decisions
• shorten new product development time
• lead to more efficient meetings.

## 2. Spoken Communication Electronically Transmitted

- Phone conversations without visual nonverbal cues
- Do have tone of voice, sender’s emphasis
- Capacity to convey extensive amounts of information
- Quick feedback ensures mutual understanding

## 3. Personally Addressed Written Communication

- Personal addressing helps ensure receiver reads it
- Cannot provide instant feedback to sender but can get feedback later
- Excellent for complex messages needing follow-up
- Includes email, text messaging and chat tools

## 4. Impersonal Written Communication

- Suited for messages that need to reach large number of receivers
- Feedback is not expected
- Newsletters, reports are examples
- **Can lead to information overload:** overabundance of information that increases the likelihood that important **information is overlooked**
