# Steps

1. Recognize the need for a decision 
2. Identify the decision criteria 
3. Generate alternatives 
4. Assess alternatives 
5. Choose an alternative 
6. Implement the chosen alternative 
7. Evaluate and learn from feedback
## Step 1

Involves two steps: 
	1. Stating the problem, and 
	2. Formulating the decision question

## Step 2

Managers must consider what variables are important

Generally, there are four categories of criteria: 
	1. legal issues 
	2. practicality and feasibility issues 
	3. ethical issues 
	4. feasibility

## Step 3

Managers must develop feasible alternative courses of action 
- If good alternatives are missed, the result may be poor 
- It is hard to develop creative alternatives, so managers need to look for new ideas
## Step 4

Four categories of criteria used to evaluate each alternative: 
	i. Practicality issues → capabilities and resources 
	ii. Economic feasibility issues → make economical sense and fit the organization’s performance goals 
	iii. Ethical issues → course of action is ethical and will not harm any stakeholder groups 
	iv. Legal issues → legal and will not violate any laws or regulations
## Step 5

Once alternatives have been evaluated, they must be ranked 
- All information needs to be considered 
- Consider using a “decision matrix” to determine the highest-ranking alternative
## Step 6

Decision must be acted upon, and many subsequent and related decisions must be made
## Step 7

Learning from feedback includes three steps: 
1. Compare what actually happened to what was expected to happen 
2. Explore why any expectations were not met 
3. Develop guidelines that will help in future decision making

# Ethics
## Codes of Ethics

Organization’s code of ethics derives from three main sources:

1. Societal Ethics → how everyone deals with each other on issues such as fairness, justice, poverty, and the rights of the individual 
2. Professional Ethics → how members of the profession make decisions when the way they should behave is not clear-cut 
3. Individual Ethics → personal standards for interacting with others, of the organization’s top managers


Managers can emphasize ethical behaviour by: 
- Ensuring ethical values and norms are central to organizational culture 
- Helping develop ethical values and standards in other employees 
- Implementing ethical control systems (codes of ethics, regular training programs, etc.) 
- Modeling ethical behaviour 

**Ethics Ombudsperson → ethics officer who monitors an organization’s practices to ensure they are ethical**

# How to Be Socially Responsible?

Impact Investing → investments that seek to solve social or environmental problems and generate financial returns to the investor 

Social Audit → allows managers to consider both the organizational and social effects of particular decisions


Sustainability → making decisions that meet needs of current generation without sacrificing future generations’ ability to do so

Sustainability strategy contains four elements: 
1. Protects the environment 
2. Promotes social responsibility 
3. Respects cultural differences 
4. Provides an economic benefit

# Decision Making in Learning Organizations

Learning Organization → One in which managers try to maximize ability of individuals and groups to think and behave creatively: 
	Creativity → ability of decision maker to discover original ideas that lead to feasible courses of action
